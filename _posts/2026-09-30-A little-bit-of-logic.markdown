Have you ever thought: Why do we need to learn math or find the "x" in high school?

I don't know how it is in your country, but here in Brazil, that is the most common complaint among teenagers. No one gives us a good reason to explain why it's so important; finding the "x" feels like it only matters just to pass school, and nothing more. We miss the bigger picture of everything we learn in the process. Every skill we develop actually helps us solve problems, understand contexts, and find solutions.

But that's OK. When we are young, we don't have much perspective on life or the experience to appreciate long-term improvement. At that stage, we aren't very rational — things like intense moments with friends are what truly motivate us.

As neuroscience explains, the brain operates with two distinct processing systems:
* **Type 1 (Automatic/Emotional):** Fast, instinctive, and guided by immediate rewards and emotions.
* **Type 2 (Deliberative/Rational):** Slow, reflective, conscious, and dependent on the prefrontal cortex.

I used to be a teenager who was good at logic because my father always showed me puzzles just for fun. I think that helped me build a good relationship with numbers. However, when I looked at the other side of school back then, I wanted nothing to do with letters, English, geography, or history... 

And that's fine too. Today, I realize life is much more flexible than we think. We spend so much time being afraid of things simply because we fear the unknown. 

Now, I finally understand how important geography, history, and English really are. It feels like my mindset shifted, and I'm a completely new person, haha. Thank God I understood logic early on, because now I just love to read and write, haha.

Just to wrap this up without a grand conclusion: I hope you can at least take away that it is never too late to learn, because everything is tomorrow ;)
